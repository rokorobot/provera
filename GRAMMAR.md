# PROVERA 1.0 — GRAMMAR

**Status:** Frozen (Closed & Stable)

This document defines the **official grammar of PROVERA 1.0**.  
The grammar is intentionally **minimal, regular, and predictable**, designed for real use in dialogue, narration, and technical contexts.

---

## 1. Sentence Structure

### Default word order
**SVO (Subject – Verb – Object)**

Examples:
- **Yo hamta ham.** — I eat food.
- **Kapa cura anak.** — The woman cares for the child.

Word order may be adjusted for emphasis, but SVO is recommended.

---

## 2. Pronouns

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|-------|----------|--------|
| 1st | yo | yoyo |
| 2nd | tu | tutu |
| 3rd (masc.) | on | onan |
| 3rd (fem.) | an | onan |
| 3rd (neut.) | en | onan |

Plural **onan** is gender‑neutral.

---

## 3. Nouns

### Gender (lexical, not inflectional)

- **-a** → feminine
- **-o** → masculine
- **no suffix** → neutral

Examples:
- **kapa** — woman
- **gama** — man (general)
- **anak** — child

### Plural

- Standard plural: **-an**  
  *anak → anakan*

- Reduplication expresses emphasis or collectivity:  
  *anak‑anak / anakanak* — many children

---

## 4. Verbs

### Verb formation

Verbs are formed as:
**root + -ta**

Examples:
- **ham → hamta** — eat
- **glok → glokta** — drink
- **facha → fachata** — work

---

### Tense (regular)

| Tense | Marker | Example |
|------|--------|---------|
| Present | -ta | hamta — eat(s) |
| Past | -tun | hamtun — ate |
| Future | -tel | hamtel — will eat |

Examples:
- **Yo hamta.** — I eat.
- **Yo hamtun.** — I ate.
- **Yo hamtel.** — I will eat.

---

### Verb *to be*

#### Short forms (spoken)

| Person | Form |
|-------|------|

| on | so |
| an | sa |
| en | se |
| yoyo | sun |

#### Regular forms (formal)

- **sota / suta / sata / seta / sunta**

Past:
- **sotun / sutun / satun / setun / suntun**

Future:
- **sotel / sutel / satel / setel / suntel**

---

## 5. Negation

- Negation is expressed with **no**, placed before the verb.

Examples:
- **Yo no hamta.** — I do not eat.
- **Tu no so hm.** — You are not fine.

---

## 6. Modality

- **musata** — must / obligation
- **bi** — conditional / would

Examples:
- **Tu musata resta.** — You must stay.
- **Yo bi mota.** — I would go.

---

## 7. Adjectives & Degree

### Position
Adjectives follow the noun.

- **hm kapa** — a nice woman

### Degree

- **hm kapa** — nice woman
- **hm kapa taj** — very nice woman
- **hm taj kapa** — nicer woman
- **hm taj taj kapa** — nicest woman

---

## 8. Prepositions

Prepositions precede the noun:

- **intra** — in / inside
- **extra** — out / outside
- **supra** — on / above
- **suba** — under
- **ad** — to / toward
- **de** — from
- **kon** — with
- **sine** — without / but (contextual)

---

## 9. Questions

Questions are formed by:
- rising intonation
- question words

Examples:
- **Come tu so?** — How are you?
- **Kam tu mota?** — Where are you going?

---

## 10. Registers

PROVERA distinguishes between registers:

- **Everyday speech** — default usage
- **Formal / technical** — procedures, reports
- **Informal / vulgar** (optional) — emotional realism

Registers do not alter core grammar.
